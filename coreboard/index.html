A board that moves like the real thing.

Referencing should be fun. Core Board now glides, pops and swings, so arranging references feels like playing with prints on a table.

  • Glide · arrange, align, undo and fit view slide things into place instead of jumping.
  • Pop · new images, notes and copies bounce in.
  • Lift & swing · a dragged image lifts off the board with a shadow and leans into your motion.
  • Flick · throw an image, or the whole board, and it coasts to a stop.

What it does

Everything a reference board should, nothing it shouldn't.

An infinite canvas with the study tools painters and artists actually reach for — and it autosaves after every change.

Infinite canvas

Drag & drop or paste images, zoom and pan freely, move and resize — layout saves itself.

Study views

Notan value steps (3 or 5 tones), saturation and detail maps, squint and grayscale — per image.

Composition guides

Rule of thirds, phi grid, golden spiral, dynamic-symmetry diagonals and center cross, over any image.

Color & palettes

Eyedrop any pixel (hex, HSV, perceptual L*) and pull the 6–10 dominant colors under each image.

Watch a folder

Point a board at your render output or screenshots folder — new images appear on the board by themselves.

Tags, search & frames

Tag items, fuzzy-search to fade everything but matches, and wrap sections in titled frames with notes.

Export with watermark

Render a selection or the whole board to one full-quality PNG, with an optional watermark.

Overlay mode

Lower the window opacity and pin it always-on-top to trace or color-match against other apps.

Align & grid pack

Snapping guides, align/distribute tools, and one-key grid packing that scales a selection to one height.

Image slots

Plan a board before you have the pictures: labeled empty slots that fill when you drop, paste or drag an image in.

Draw on images

Arrows, circles, boxes and pen marks in marker colors. They stick to the picture through moves, crops and rotations.

Lock & pin

Lock items so they can't be nudged, or pin a reference to the screen while the board moves underneath.

Focus view

One image big on a dark backdrop. Arrow through the whole board like a slideshow, zoom into details, study it, and pop back.

Paste a web link

Copy an image address, or a whole ArtStation or museum page, and press Ctrl+V. The picture downloads straight onto the board.

Mini-map

An overview of the whole board in the corner while you're zoomed in. Click or drag in it to get anywhere fast.

Surprise me

Press S for a random reference from any of your boards. Rediscover old finds or pick a warm-up study.

Coming from PureRef?

A free PureRef alternative, with study tools built in.

If you've used PureRef, you'll feel at home in a minute. Then Core Board goes further: it helps you study your references, not just pin them up.

Feels familiar

  • Infinite canvas: drag & drop or paste images, pan and zoom
  • Always on top and window opacity over your painting or 3D app
  • Flip, rotate, grayscale and crop, without changing your files
  • Notes, frames and grid arrange to keep boards tidy
  • Saves itself after every change; several boards, one click apart

Then goes further

  • Value, notan, saturation and squint views on any image (V)
  • Composition guides: thirds, golden spiral, diagonals (C)
  • Color palettes pulled from images, and a picker that shows value
  • Focus view, drawing on images, captions and image slots
  • Find similar pictures, watch a folder, and paste web links

Core Board can't open PureRef (.pur) files yet: drag your images in, or point Watch folder at your reference folder. PureRef is a trademark of its owner; Core Board is an independent app and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by PureRef.

Get it running

Download, double-click, done.

One-click installers for Windows and macOS, and a package for Linux. No account, no admin password. The first run creates a fresh board.

Install it

  1. Windows · download CoreBoard-Setup-0.3.3.exe, double-click it and click Install. Core Board opens when it's done and lands in the Start menu and on your desktop. If Windows says "Windows protected your PC", click More info → Run anyway (it isn't code-signed yet).
  2. macOS · open the .dmg and drag Core Board onto Applications. The first time, right-click the app → Open (or System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway), since it isn't notarized yet.
  3. Linux · double-click the .deb (Ubuntu, Debian, Mint…), or use the .tar.gz and run ./coreboard.

Prefer no installer on Windows? The portable .zip runs from anywhere: unzip and double-click coreboard.exe.

First steps

Once it's open:

  1. Drop images onto the canvas (or Ctrl+V to paste).
  2. Arrange · drag to move, corner handle to resize, scroll to zoom.
  3. Press Ctrl+O to create or switch boards, or watch a folder.
  4. Hit V for study views, D to draw, H for the help overlay.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is Core Board really free?

Yes, completely. No price, no subscription, no account, no ads and no watermark on your exports unless you add your own.

Is it a good PureRef alternative?

If you use PureRef to collect and arrange references, Core Board does the same basics: an infinite canvas, drag and drop or paste, always on top, window opacity, flip, rotate, grayscale and crop. On top of that it has study tools built in: value, notan and saturation views, composition guides, color palettes, focus view and drawing on images.

Can it open my PureRef (.pur) boards?

Not yet. For now, drag your reference images onto a Core Board board, or point Watch folder at the folder where you keep them and they'll appear by themselves.

Which computers does it run on?

Windows 10 and 11, macOS 11 or newer (Apple Silicon and Intel Macs), and 64-bit Linux (a .deb for Ubuntu, Debian and Mint, or a .tar.gz for other distributions).

Windows or macOS warns me when I open it. Is it safe?

The warning appears because Core Board isn't code-signed yet, which costs money every year. On Windows, click More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac, right-click the app and choose Open, or go to System Settings, Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway. You only need to do this once.

Does it change my original images?

No. Images are copied into the board, and crops, flips, rotations, study views and drawings are all saved separately, so you can undo or reset them at any time.

Where are my boards saved?

Each board is a normal folder with your images and a small board file inside, so it's easy to back up or copy to another computer. Boards save themselves after every change. Press Ctrl+O to create, open and switch boards.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Everything works offline. Only pasting a web link and searching the web for more pictures need an internet connection, and Core Board doesn't send anything about you or your boards anywhere.

Can I keep it on top of Photoshop, Krita or Blender?

Yes. Press T to keep the board always on top, and [ and ] to make the window more see-through, so your references float over the app you're working in.

How do I update to a new version?

Download the latest version from this page and install it over the old one. Your boards are kept. Every past version stays available under Releases.

I found a bug or have an idea. Where do I send it?

Through the contact page. Bug reports and feature ideas are very welcome.
